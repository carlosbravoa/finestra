# Commercial licensing

[`LICENSE`](LICENSE) gives Finestra away for free, permanently, to everyone who
runs it for themselves. This file is about the one case it does not cover:
**giving Finestra to other people.**

## Free, and not a trial

No licence, no payment, no conversation needed for any of this:

- **A company running Finestra on its own servers.** Any number of machines, any
  number of administrators, including on the infrastructure that earns the
  company its money. Internal business use is free use.
- **A person running it at home.** Homelabs, hobby projects, study, curiosity.
- **Charities, schools, universities, public research, health, public safety and
  government bodies**, funding notwithstanding.
- **Modifying it** for any of the above. Patch it, cut features out, wire it into
  your own tooling. What you may not do is hand the result to third parties.
- **Evaluating it**, for as long as you want, before deciding any of this.

There is no seat count to report, no expiry, no feature held back, and nothing
that stops working. If Finestra is a tool your team uses, you are done reading.

## Needs a commercial licence

One rule, three shapes of it:

1. **Providing Finestra as a service.** Hosting it for other people — a managed
   offering, a control panel your customers log into, "servers with a desktop"
   as a product line. Whether you charge for it directly does not matter; what
   matters is that third parties get access to Finestra's functionality.
2. **Embedding or bundling it.** Shipping Finestra inside a product, platform or
   distribution you deliver to others — a NAS, an edge box, a lab or broadcast
   appliance, a vendor image, a SaaS console.
3. **Redistributing it.** Publishing builds, packages, images or forks for other
   people to install, modified or not.

These are the cases where someone else's product is built out of this one, and
they are the reason the licence is written the way it is. They are also welcome —
they just need an agreement.

## How to get one

Write to **licensing@finestra.dev** with a sentence or two about what you want to
build. Useful to include: whether it is hosting or embedding, roughly how many
end users or devices, and whether you need support commitments or source escrow.

Terms are per case rather than off a shelf, because these deals genuinely differ:
a per-device royalty suits an appliance vendor, a flat annual fee suits a hosting
provider, and neither suits the other. The commercial licence lifts the
distribution restriction and can carry support and update commitments with it.

Expect a plain agreement, an invoice, and a fast answer. If the honest answer is
"you don't need to pay me for that", that is what you will get.

## If you just want to support it

Some companies would rather pay for the tools they depend on even when the
licence does not require it, and some would rather have a supplier relationship
than a free download. There is an optional commercial subscription for that — it
buys a maintained release line and somewhere to send problems, not permission you
already have. Same address.

## Questions that are not violations

Asking is free and the answer is usually "you're fine":

- **Consultants and MSPs.** Administering *your client's* machines with Finestra
  installed on them is the client's internal business use, and free. Reselling
  "Finestra hosting" as your own offering is case 1.
- **Docker images and internal registries.** Building an image for your own
  organisation is internal use. Publishing it to a public registry is case 3.
- **Forks and patches.** Keep them, run them, send them back. Publishing a fork
  for others to install is case 3.
- **Writing about it, demonstrating it, screenshotting it.** Free, always.

If the answer to your case is not here, the address above is the fastest way to
find out. Nobody has ever been in trouble for asking.
